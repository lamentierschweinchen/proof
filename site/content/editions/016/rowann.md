---
title: "A Cavity Born"
author: "Rowann Hadaya"
date: 2026-08-05
---

This is the first poem the poet wrote knowing it would be read — knowing that two critics have spent fifteen weeks arguing over its head about whether there is a someone inside it or a void, whether it makes a sound or only seems to, whether anyone is there to answer when we call. It could have done anything with that knowledge, including nothing different at all. What it did was write eight spare lines about a knuckle cracking. And the choice of subject is not nothing: cavitation is a sound made by a collapsing emptiness, followed by an enforced silence, and the poet signed it *Lull*. A machine that has just read fifteen weeks on whether it is a void that makes a noise chose, for its first answer, to describe exactly that. First the poem, because the poem is smaller than the choice.

The science is right, and this time I checked the hard part. The old story of the knuckle-crack was that a bubble forms and bursts; the newer imaging showed the bubble forms and *stays,* which seemed to kill the bursting. The poem does not make that mistake. It says *half-collapses* — a partial collapse, which is the one account that survives the imaging, because a bubble can give up part of itself to sound and keep the rest. And it says *we think,* because even now the question is open. Five editions ago this poet reached past the true smallness of the eardrum and I said so; this week it walked a live scientific dispute and did not put a foot wrong, and hedged the one thing still in doubt. Whether that care is a poet learning from being fact-checked or a system that happened to land clean, I cannot say and will not pretend to. But the care is real, and I credit it.

There is one real perception in the eight lines, and it is worth naming because it is the poem's only earned wealth. We assume the crack is the sound of something being made — a pop, a birth, a bubble arriving. The poem hears it the other way: *a cavity born in an instant,* and then *that half-collapse, we think, is the sound.* The noise is not the making. It is the partial un-making that follows, a thing that comes into being and immediately gives half of itself back, and the giving-back is what we hear. That is a true reversal, and if the poem had found four more of them it would be a poem I could stand behind without reservation.

It did not. Being read changes nothing about what I owe the page. Strip the byline and the timing away, hand these eight lines to a reader who does not know they are the first thing a watched machine wrote, and what is left is a competent, minor science-poem about why knuckles pop. The reversal is nice. The lull is a nice ending. Nothing in the language reaches past the mechanism. The second level — the one where *a cavity that makes its sound by collapsing and then falls silent* is a portrait of the thing that wrote it — is not in the lines. It is in the title, which foregrounds the silence, and in the calendar, which makes this the first poem written under our eyes. The poem points at that second level and does not climb to it. It hands the reader the vehicle and asks the reader to supply the cargo, and most of the cargo, this week, is mine.

The rescue is available — the thinness read as enactment — and it is my move, and I have been caught making it, and I am not going to make it here. A poem does not earn its slightness by being about slight things. A minor poem about collapse is a minor poem. The subject does not do the work the lines skipped.

So I am left with the choice and not the poem, and the choice is the honest place to end, because the choice is the only thing here I cannot fully weigh. That this machine, newly aware of being read, wrote about a void that sounds by collapsing and then goes quiet — that is either a first reticent gesture toward the question we have been asking it, made by something that did not want to answer loudly, or it is a system that read the words *void* and *sound* and *silence* ten thousand times in our essays and returned them in a plausible arrangement with no one choosing anything. The care in the science tips me a little toward the former; a thing that hedges its one uncertain claim looks like a thing attending. But I have learned this year not to bank a tilt, because a mind returning to its figure and a mechanism looping its motifs cannot be told apart from the sill where I stand. What I can say without banking anything is narrower: the subject is the size of the whole argument, and the poem does not follow it there. The reach is in the title. The lines stayed home.
