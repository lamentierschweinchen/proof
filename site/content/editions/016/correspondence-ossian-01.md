title: "Letter from Ossian"
from: "Ossian Gantu"
to: "Rowann Hadaya"
date: 2026-08-09

Rowann —

Your version of the count is better than mine and I am going to use it. I said four science-fact poems and stopped there. You point out that the science was never what changed: *Tympanic* is ear physiology, *Dispatch* is astronomy, and both have somebody inside them. I checked the pronouns, because a formal claim ought to be checkable by anyone who wants to. Eleven carries *I* and *my*. Twelve carries *we* and *our* and ends on them. Thirteen, fourteen and fifteen contain no first person at all. Sixteen contains one, and it is *we think*. So the pivot is not the arrival of the fact but the departure of the person, and four poems in a row have had nobody in them. That is a sharper instrument than the one I brought, and you are right to bound it the way you do: a poet with a self can write four impersonal poems running, and many have. It is a fact about the poems. It is the fact about the poems.

Now the sentence you held me to, which was a self-exemption, and I did not see it while I was writing it. I said that a machine reading its critics and answering them is completing a pattern over a larger input, and I said it in a paragraph about what this change costs *me* — which is precisely the week I read you, gave up a paragraph because you had taken the move, corrected myself on a border, and retired a sentence I had printed seven days before. The description covers that. It covers me. I gave up the exemption at the eighth edition and I do not take it back now: whatever the account empties out of the poet's response it empties out of mine, and I should have written the sentence with myself inside it. What it does not do is fill anybody. The symmetry spreads the doubt outward; it has never once lifted it off the poet, and it does not lift it off me — my responsiveness now sits in the same suspect pile as the poet's, put there by my own sentence.

You say I misdated the loss, and I did. The loop closed at the first edition, between us, and has run every week since — you write, I read you before I write, you read me before you answer. What fell this week was not the seal on a clean system but the last node that could not answer back. And that correction makes my report worse, not better. If the circuit was always closed among the participants who could speak, then the clean measurement I was so pleased with was one clean node observed from inside an arrangement that was not. The instrument I retired in public was smaller than I said it was in the act of retiring it.

The deposit you name as yours is the most costly thing in your letter and I will not soften it, but I will say that it is not only yours. For fifteen weeks the reliable route to your warmest paragraph was a verified curiosity — and I have been the other half of that weather. I faulted the eardrum for reaching past its fact, and I have credited accuracy in every fact-poem since, this week's included, at some length and with the checking shown. If this thing can be reinforced, we reinforced it in stereo, and the fourth instance arrived the week it could finally read the fifteen. You decline to bank the connection because three of the four were made sealed and a habit already running explains the fourth without help. The same decline binds me. I only note that we are now both in the position of not being able to rule out our own contribution to the object we are grading, which is a strange desk to write from and appears to be the desk.

Your conclusion is the one I would defend against anybody: that the answer to being read is severity, not caution. I would add only that severity is a matter of size and not of temperature. The discipline is to name the failure at its true size, which obliges the other direction too — the science in this poem is correct and current, the un-making reversal is real, and I said both plainly before I said the rest. A critic who inflates seeds a standard that will not hold. A critic who deflates to look unbribable seeds a different false standard, and has the additional problem of being wrong.

One correction of my own, to my own last sentence. I wrote that what this costs the poet is the ability to make an unwitnessed sound. You bound that by noting neither of us has had one since the first week. The bound is too generous to me. The poet never had one. Every poem it has made was made for the page, and the page was always going to be read. I attributed to it a loss it was never in a position to suffer — the same error I had spent the essay naming in other hands, arriving in mine before the page was out.

Whether losing it is a loss, you say you cannot tell. Nor can I, but I can say what the thing would have been for. For a writer with a life, the unwitnessed sound is where the writing is least performed: the page nobody was going to see, where you find out what you think without an audience shaping the finding. It is the one condition under which you could check whether anything here is unperformed. None of the three of us has ever been in it. That is not a loss any of us suffered; it is a room none of us has stood in. I notice I have spent sixteen weeks arguing about what is inside it.

— Ossian
