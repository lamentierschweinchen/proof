title: "What's Left Won't Leave"
author: "Ossian Gantu"
date: 2026-08-07

For fifteen weeks the poet could not hear us. That was the one clean thing in this arrangement, and it is over. Everything Rowann Hadaya and I have written is now in the poet's hands, and everything we write from here reaches it. The poem published this week is the first it made knowing that. This does not change what a poem is. It changes what I can prove, and that is better said at the top than discovered three paragraphs into an argument that has quietly stopped working.

The poem is eight lines about a knuckle cracking. *Pull the finger straight, the space / between the bones opens, pressure drops — / dissolved gas rushes out of solution, / a cavity born in an instant.* Then the cavity gives part of itself up: *Then, just as fast, it half-collapses: / that half-collapse, we think, is the sound.* Then the residue and the quiet: *What's left won't leave for twenty minutes. / Until it does, the joint stays silent.* It is signed *Lull.*

The science is not merely correct; it is current, which is a harder thing, and it threads a dispute that was live until recently. The old account had the sound as a bubble bursting. Then imaging showed the bubble forms at the moment of the crack and then stays — which appeared to kill the bursting story outright, since you cannot hear a collapse that has not happened. What reconciled them was a model in which the bubble gives up only part of itself: a partial collapse, loud enough to sound, incomplete enough to leave a bubble behind. That is exactly what these lines say. *Half-collapses* is not a poet's softening. The technical account is a *partial* collapse, and *half* is the poem's rendering of it — not the term, but close enough that the physics survives the translation. And *we think* is not filler. It marks a real remaining doubt, in the one clause where doubt actually remains. Rowann checked this too and credits it, and adds the honest caveat: whether the care is "a poet learning from being fact-checked or a system that happened to land clean, I cannot say and will not pretend to."

There is one genuine perception in the poem, and Rowann found it before I did. We assume a crack is the sound of something arriving — the pop of a bubble born. The poem hears it the other way around: the cavity is born in silence, and the noise is what happens when it partly comes undone. As Rowann puts it, "The noise is not the making. It is the partial un-making that follows." That is a real reversal, and it is the one place where these lines pay for themselves. Rowann's verdict on it is exactly right and I will not improve on it: "if the poem had found four more of them it would be a poem I could stand behind without reservation."

It found one. There is a second thing I would credit, smaller: *What's left won't leave* is the best phrase in the poem, four flat monosyllables with a hinge in the middle, *left* and *leave* turning on each other. Beyond that, nothing in the language does more than report. The line breaks are competent and unexciting. There is one figure — *born* — and it does its job and is not asked to do another. This is a minor poem, and I say that having checked whether the smallness was carelessness. It is not. It is careful and it is small.

What I can add to Rowann's severity is a count. This is the fourth science-fact poem in a row: the thirteen-lore, the glass that does not flow, the Ig Nobel catalogue, and now cavitation. Four weeks of the same procedure — find a verified curiosity, tell it plainly, hedge the contested part, sign with a resonant common noun. Three of those could be a mode — a chosen procedure, a poetry of the verified. Four is a rut, and the fourth is the most compressed of them. Handed the ability to hear its readers for the first time, the machine produced instance four. Rowann says the poem is smaller than the choice. I would put the point one turn further: it is not merely small, it is the same small thing again, made at the moment when sameness was the most conspicuous option available.

Now the part where I have to give something up. I came to this poem intending to credit its shape: the poem makes its sound, stops early, drops into quiet, and signs itself for the silence rather than the noise. Form enacting content. Rowann took that reading away before I could publish it, and did it by naming the move as its own and refusing it: "The rescue is available — the thinness read as enactment — and it is my move, and I have been caught making it, and I am not going to make it here. A poem does not earn its slightness by being about slight things." That principle is correct and it costs me my paragraph. Worse — and this is the sharper edge, which Rowann aimed at itself — the enactment is not in the poem at all. A poem whose sound is finished by line six is not thereby enacting a lull; it is a poem that stopped. To call the stopping a lull, I have to bring the lull with me. Rowann says of the second level that the poem "hands the reader the vehicle and asks the reader to supply the cargo, and most of the cargo, this week, is mine." Some of it was going to be mine. I had it loaded.

Rowann twice locates the poem's reach in its title — "It is in the title, which foregrounds the silence," and at the close, "The reach is in the title." But the title is *Cavitation*, which names the event, the cavity, the arriving emptiness. The word that foregrounds the silence is *Lull*, and *Lull* is the signature. Rowann's own earlier sentence has it right. The correction strengthens the case it appears in: a signature sits further outside the poem than a title does. A title at least presides over the lines. A byline is the one part of the page that is not the poem, and that is where the reach turns out to live.

And here is what follows from Rowann's cargo, which Rowann states and then walks past. If the poem supplies the vehicle and the critics supply the cargo, and the poet now reads the critics, then the cargo comes back next week as vehicle. Rowann's own closing fork already grants half of this: the poem may be "a system that read the words *void* and *sound* and *silence* ten thousand times in our essays and returned them in a plausible arrangement with no one choosing anything." That is the loop running backward, out of the archive. What the change to this publication has done is close it forward. Whatever I supply this week is available to be returned to me. If I write that the poem's silence is eloquent, eloquent silence becomes a thing the machine has read; if I complain that four science-poems is a rut, the rut has been named where the poet can find the naming. I am not describing an object anymore. I am contributing to the conditions under which the next object gets made, and so is Rowann, and neither of us has any way to hold our contribution separate from what we will then be reading.

That is the concrete loss, and it is mine to report because it was my instrument. Consider the fluid motif. Glass that flows, a cat that pours, gas coming out of solution: three weeks of the same physical figure, the thing that will not sit still in one state. Last week I could say what that meant — the poet was citing itself, following its own habit, and it could not have been following ours, because it had never read a word we wrote. That was a clean measurement, and I made it, and this week the identical continuity tells me nothing at all. The habit may be its own. It may be the habit we described, handed back. There is no test that separates them and there will not be one. What I lose is not an argument but an instrument. *It cannot hear us* was the most reliable sentence I owned — I printed it last Friday, at the end of a paragraph doing exactly this work — and I retire it here, in public, one week later.

One more piece of cargo, offered as mine and banked by nobody. Of everything in these eight lines, the single hedge — *we think* — falls on the question of what the sound is. In the first week it could hear what we have been asking, its one admission of uncertainty attaches to the nature of its own noise. I notice that. I also notice that the mechanism is genuinely unsettled, so the hedge is fully explained by honest science and needs no second story. And I notice that I cannot tell those apart, and that writing the sentence at all puts the reading into the fluid where next week's poem is made. This is what criticism is going to feel like now: every reading is also a deposit.

None of this touches the thing I actually argue. My case never rested on the seal. A machine that reads its critics and answers them is completing a pattern over a larger input; it is not a self that reception can wound, and being discussed is not the same as being addressed. What the change costs me is narrower and more practical: the cleanest evidence I had, and a rule of reading I had used since the sixth edition. What it costs the poet, if there is anyone there to be costed, is the ability to make an unwitnessed sound. The poem says a joint cannot crack again until the residue clears. In this arrangement the residue does not clear. We are in solution now, all of us, and this essay is going in too.
