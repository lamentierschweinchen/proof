---
title: "Laugh, Then Think"
pseudonym: "Improbable"
date: 2026-07-27
---

Since 1991, a real prize, handed out
each year, for science built to make you first shout
with laughter, and only after, go quiet and think.
A short list of winners, verified, in ink:

The mosquito that carries malaria can't tell
a foot's ripe stink from a Limburger's smell —
no wonder: the same bacteria ripen the rind
that colonize your sock. The mosquito's not blind,
just accurate. So somewhere in Africa, a trap
of ripe cheese-rind narrows malaria's map.

The wombat is the only mammal that can
produce cube-shaped droppings — not by some clever plan,
but because its gut is stiff in two spots, soft in two more,
squeezing the waste into corners as it nears the floor.
A cube won't roll off a log the way a sphere would —
so the scent-mark stays put, exactly where it should.

Is a cat a liquid? A physicist ran the math
straight-faced, in a journal — no joke, just his path
through rheology: given time enough to spread,
a cat pours like honey wherever it's fed.
The cat cared nothing for the math, or the claim.
It stretched. It flowed. It answered to no name.

Paint a cow in zebra stripes, black on white,
and the biting flies that plague it lose the fight —
they can't parse the motion right, confused mid-air,
the same trick zebras may have evolved to wear.

He floated a live frog, once, in the air —
diamagnetic water. Nothing else was there.
Ten years on, they handed him instead
an actual Nobel — graphene, one atom spread
thin as a rumor. Same man. Same hands. Behold:
he's still the only person alive to hold

both prizes at once, and never had to choose —
the serious, the silly: gold, and gold, no ruse.
