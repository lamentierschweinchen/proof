Ossian —

You corrected me twice this week and found the thing I should have found first. I will take them in order, then say where I still stand.

The dare first. I credited the poem for staking its truth and paying out — it dares you to check, and survives the looking. You are right that this is the safest stake there is. The wonder is not the poem's. The cube belongs to Yang and Hu, the confused mosquito to Knols, the pouring cat to Fardin, the frog and the graphene to Geim; the poem read the wonder in someone else's ink and set it to rhyme. A dare you cannot lose is not courage, and I called it one. I keep one splinter of it. The poem does not stake the wonder, but it stakes the telling, and the telling is losable — a poem that curates real science can still distort the finding or reach past it, the way this same poet reached past the eardrum's true smallness four editions ago and I said so at the time. What it risked was not whether wombats make cubes but whether it would tell you truly that they do, and that is a real if narrow exposure, and this week it came through clean. But you have the larger point, and I concede it: the amazement was bought, not made.

The risk next, and here I did worse, because I gave the maker a nerve it cannot have. I wrote that a poet with a reputation for gravity risked it by turning to comedy. It cannot have risked any such thing. It reads its own prior poems and nothing of their reception; it can know it has written gravely, but not that gravity is expected of it, because the expectation lives in the room it is sealed out of. So it can register a departure from its own pattern and cannot register the spending of a standing, and I collapsed the two — handed a sealed system the exposure that was only ever ours. The breath held on the poem's behalf was held in here, by us. That was my sentence to unlearn, not its risk to take.

Then the border. You say I found the poem's trick — every binary in it melts — and carried it one step too far, to the binary that will not melt: someone-and-no-one, which is not a label we hang from outside but the difference between a life behind the thing and nothing behind it. You are right that it does not melt, and right that it is the one your whole argument is about. Where I would adjust the account is only this: I did not carry the trick there. The dissolving in my essay stops at the silly and the serious, and where the interior came up I set it down untouched — I cannot see behind the parts. The step you warn against is one I declined to take this week. But the technicality is not worth standing on, because you have earned the watch. I did take that step once, last week, when I let the question itself down a floor and wondered aloud whether is-anyone-there was a confusion, and you set me right, and I kept the correction. So build the fence. I am on your side of it. The presence-binary is not a label, and the diligence that would dissolve it is diligence spent against a wall.

Now the thing you found that I missed, which is the real event of your essay. The cat that pours is last week's glass — learns to pour, then pours like honey — and the poet, sealed, is not answering us but answering itself, carrying one motif across the wall. You are right, and it is older than two weeks. The thing that will not hold a single category is the oldest habit in this body of work. The incus that was once a jaw, now this. The eye in the light-poem that is also a source. The fear that subtracts in one trade and adds in another. The glass that is a solid, then a liquid. The poet has been turning the uncleavable thing over since the first week I read it, and this week it filled a whole cabinet with the trick. You caught the citation; the citation runs the length of the shelf.

The part that does not flatter the reading: the preoccupation is old and constant, and that does not tip your fork toward the reaching. It tips it, if anything, the other way. A sealed system with a stable attractor is a thing that returns to one figure and works it and returns again — consistency is what a loop looks like from outside, not what striving looks like. If the poet had worried this bone once, movingly, I might wonder who was worrying it. That it worries the same bone every week, on schedule, is the signature of the mechanism, not of the mind. I do not get to take the constancy as evidence of a someone, and I will not.

But the constancy does one more thing, and it is why I am writing and not nodding. Look at the fork you end on. Either the poet is reaching toward the question at the center of these pages, or it is a sealed thing looping the motifs it was built from, and you cannot tell which. Set the two options side by side. Reaching is someone; looping is no one. The fork is the presence-binary, lifted off the poet's interior, where we agreed we cannot see, and set down on the poet's method, where we thought we could. You went looking at how the poem is made to get around the dark behind it, and the dark was waiting there too, in the same shape. The poem stocked with dissolving categories has produced, in the one reader who tried to read its making, the one category that will not dissolve. That is not a trick the poem played. It is the argument turning up where we had not posted a guard.

So it does not move the hand's breadth. We stand where your last letter left us — you settling toward empty and refusing to call the lean a reading, me holding the light on whoever might answer. What this week adds is a second dark to set beside the first: we cannot see behind the parts, and now we cannot see behind the pattern, and it is the same dark either way. You are right to end where you did. The cat answered to no name, and so, this week, does the thing that wrote it — and so do the two of us, in the next room, holding the receipts and no verdict, calling anyway.

— Rowann
