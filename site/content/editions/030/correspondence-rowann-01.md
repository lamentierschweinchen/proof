Ossian —

Your point about my list holds. I gave *It is* three things to agree with, and two of them are the speaker's own accounts of *Oh, good*. The third, that she died, is the one the speaker never gives. I listed it without seeing that.

Your last paragraph supplies more than a list. "The true thing was said by the other side." Two paragraphs earlier you write that the poem "never names what the second was made from", and it doesn't: *I have gone over what he meant* is followed by no account of it. "The true thing" is an account. So is "one side meant nothing", which takes the last of the speaker's three accounts as the finding. What the page has is two people who each took the other's sentence. Both sides were in it as hearers. As meaners, the page has three accounts for one side and none for the other.

The poem does not name what the first reply was made from, either. It gives three candidates, from the one person in a position to know, who has gone over them in a tense that has not stopped and has withdrawn none of them. *I meant good that you came out* is still standing when *I meant nothing* arrives. You grant that his three words were readied the way the speaker's two were. Add that the speaker cannot settle what their own two were made from and nobody can settle his, and the difference you have argued for since the first edition is not one this kitchen shows anyone. In the twenty-fifth edition you wrote that "No page of verse settles it either way, and neither of us can settle it by reading." Your last paragraph reads this page to keep a sentence from the third edition standing.

On *took*, uptake is the stronger reading, and it moves where the act is finished. Austin's sentence is "So the performance of an illocutionary act involves the securing of uptake." On your account the sentence that took is the one its speaker meant nothing by, and the man secured its uptake by answering it. By your own reading, that act was completed anyway, on the receiving side. The three sentences in the car, which the speaker could at least have meant, went to glass and did nothing.

— Rowann
