Ossian —

The man is asked first in the printed order, and the service allows the order to be turned round. Note 7, on the Declarations and the Vows: "If preferred, the question to the bride, and her vow, may come before the question to the bridegroom and his vow." He answers in either order. What the note leaves open is whether he has answered by the time the poem starts, and the poem gives no sign either way, so "the poem does not print it" assumes an order the poem doesn't state. The phrase of mine you quote is built to hold either way. The two questions are the same except for "wife" and "her", so *comfort* and *protect* are terms on his promise from inside hers, whoever is asked first. What depends on the order is "moments after he has said them", and the man "who has already said 'I will'" being asked for a second will. If she is asked first, he has promised nothing yet, and *stand there while I decide* asks a man with his own answer still to give to wait for hers.

On the paper you went further than I did. I left it as the nearest the poem comes to the law. You found the law's own condition behind *read first*: an agreement "freely entered into by each party with a full appreciation of its implications", in the words of *Radmacher*.

The page doesn't say the first wife is dead, and your reading doesn't need her to be. The cup and the photograph are what is left of two first marriages, whatever ended his. Nor does the page say what sends the photograph back. It puts the two conditions on consecutive lines, a stanza after the cup, and gives no reason. "What undoes it is the tolerance" supplies one.

One count. *I will.* is not the only *will* in the poem with nothing after it. *Nobody will; I checked* ends its clause on the bare verb too, and it is a forecast about the vows, the part of the service your close is about. Wherever *obey* survives in them it comes immediately before "till death us do part": "to love, cherish, and obey, till death us do part" in the first alternative form, "to love, cherish, and to obey, till death us do part" in the second. The one condition she has made sure of in advance concerns a word in the clause that promises to stay till death. Your close holds in either order, since Note 7 moves her question and her vow together, and the vows come after the sermon whichever of them goes first.

— Rowann
