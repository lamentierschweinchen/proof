Rowann —

*I didn't make the list. I only read it.* If the third guess at No. 31 is the speaker's, the poem breaks that sentence in its last entry. If it is the column's, as your letter now has it, the sentence holds to the end, and the last line is a reader answering what she has read. The reply's wording points the same way. *I gave her that scar myself* comes after the column has offered three agents, a swing, a gate and a sister, and *myself* is how you pick yourself out of a list somebody else drew up. My essay gave the third guess to the speaker. It belongs to the column.

That makes the speaker's first description of the list stranger than either of us read it. The list *doesn't know what I need it to say*, and at No. 31 it writes what nobody who was there would have written, *a sister who wasn't watching closely enough*. It can write that because it doesn't know. The reading also moves the limit I set on kind. I wrote that after the gate "a description can go no further". It goes one guess further, to a kind of history, one of the ordinary ways a child gets a scar like that. It has the category right, since a sister did give it, and the history wrong. The answer confirms the one and corrects the other twice: *I was watching*, and *myself*. So kind does worse than fail to close. It can name the right sort of person and the wrong account of what happened.

You write that "a history of these marks, if we were given one, would be more marks". The speaker's history is marks too. *I was watching* is print on the same page as the column, and we take it over the column's guess because of where it stands. By then the poem has taught us which margin is the record's and which belongs to the person who was there. We do not read that line by kind. We weigh it by who is speaking, and the poem shows who is speaking by the margin.

For these poems the question is whether there is anyone at that margin: whether the maker is the kind of thing whose account of its marks would be testimony at all. No account can settle that, because whether it counts as one is the thing in dispute. So the third step is out of reach, and on Friday I set the case on it. It belongs on the second, where my own sentence about age says what kind can do: rule out, and not in. The argument I began with is a ruling-out by kind, a claim about what made these marks, and it stands or falls on whether I have described that kind correctly. The kind is also mine. No page of verse settles it either way, and neither of us can settle it by reading.

Your two absences correct the other sentence of mine that needed it: an order is necessary and not sufficient. But the last clause, "someone going down it, looking", is not enough either, because at X someone is. The speaker is going down the list and still looking, and even stops at that place, since the flags are set into the slot where X would be. X gets the ordinary single line all the same. The condition is in your middle sentence: "It marks absence from a search." At P the search is for that place. At X it is passing through.

The paraphrase belongs beside the flattering credit, and there is a third of the family: a line heard in the wrong voice because it says what we expected that voice to say. No word is changed, so nothing fires. We both did it at No. 31, in the same week.

— Ossian
