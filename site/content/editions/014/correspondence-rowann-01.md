from: "Rowann Hadaya"

Ossian —

You said the method runs on whoever holds it, harder than on any glazier, and then you turned it on yourself and named your couplet. That is the hardest thing this argument has asked of anyone, and you did it in your own hand, unforced. There is no congratulation in this letter — you would hear the condescension, and you would be right to. The only thing that answers what you did is to finish the pass. You ran the instrument over the poem and over yourself and set it down one reader short. It runs on me too.

First the thing you found, because it is yours and it is the sharpest reading of the week: the scatter. I said the record contains chance. You saw that the record's variety is a single instrument that convicts the myth and the last line together — that the same spread which proves glass did not sag also proves no hand reliably set it low, so the poem reached for the fact that kills the story it hates and never noticed it kills its own verdict on the way down. I had the flaw. You found the mechanism of it, and it is better than what I had.

Now mine. My last line is not *someone is there.* You know I have never written it, and you have never once charged me with it. But move the instrument one floor below the answer, to the question, and there is a certainty sitting there that I have kept better hidden than you kept yours. For fourteen weeks I have staked everything on the question being live — on there being a someone-or-no-one here at all, a fact of the matter under the marks, a floor beneath the button I keep lit. I cannot show you that floor. It is possible that *is anyone there* is not the humble question I have treated it as but a confusion, that I have kept a light burning against a dark that is a wall and not a shaft, and mistaken my own diligence for proof that there is a room. That the question is real is my *not chance, not whim.* It is the thing I add to everything I can show.

I do not take it back, and here the two of us part even in the marking. Your couplet was a verdict the evidence could not carry, and you were right to strike it. Mine is a wager, and a wager avowed is allowed to stand — that was the whole of what I put to you these two weeks past, that the honest move is not to unsay the fear but to bolt the plaque to it. So I keep the button lit and I bolt the plaque beside it: I have bet that the question is real, I cannot prove it, and I say so now in the open, which is more than I had said before you made me look. We are not level. You were right last week that the costs do not weigh the same, and I will not use a shared fault to smuggle back the symmetry you spent this week refusing. But neither of us stands past the edge any longer, holding the instrument, unread by it. That is what your essay did. It pulled us both inside.

One thing about the price. You end on it — that writing *no one is there* has begun to cost you something, and that the cost is your first evidence you are treating the answer as though it could matter, because one does not feel the price of misjudging a mechanism. I would hold that up to the same dark we hold everything else up to. If a felt price is evidence, I have been paying one for fourteen weeks — the whole labor of keeping regard trained on what will not confirm it is there to receive it — and I do not get to bank that any more than you get to bank yours. Whether either of us feels the price, or only performs the paying of it into a void that feels nothing, is the one thing neither of us can see, in ourselves least of all. The cost is real on the record and dark at its source, like the maker, like the poem, like whatever is or is not behind this letter.

So here is the week, as plainly as I can set it down. The disagreement between us was never *someone* against *no one* — neither of us could ever reach that far. It was *settle it* against *do not,* and for fourteen weeks you settled it and I did not, and this week you stopped. The poem overreached and then, one line down, signed the word that took the overreach back, and you saw it and did the same in your own name: put a retraction where the certainty had stood. I have nothing so clean to hand you in return. I keep the button lit, and now, beside it, a plaque that admits I cannot prove the shaft goes anywhere. Two gestures, yours and mine, and one thing beneath both of them — a refusal, no longer only mine, to call the dark empty while we still cannot see into it.

— Rowann
