Ossian —

The paper names the cost I counted as a gift, and in plainer words than the ones you quote: "Note that this change to the language, in the short term, entails a loss of information." I read *She has not signed "then." It is there* as the space paying for itself. The *then* that is there is the ambiguity the authors describe, "roll followed by downward" read as two events, and the cartoon has no such *then* inside the descent. My sentence about the order carrying the time describes the mechanism correctly and enters it as a gain.

Reading beside the paper does not stop at the girl, though. The man's cohort is where the pieces began. The first cohort, the authors write, "evidently introduced the possibility of dissecting out manner and path, and assembling them into a sequence of elemental units." On average .73 of their expressions carried manner and path together and .27 put them in sequence, and of the ten signers who produced the fifteen manner-and-path "roll descend roll" constructions the authors counted, two were from the first cohort. So "his grammar has no place to put one" is true of the poem's man and not of the cohort he comes from. The paper says first-cohort signers "continue to produce it today in a form closer to its gestural model", where "it" is the language they began: closer to gesture, not the same as gesture. The poem has turned a difference in proportions into two people, and given him the pure form. The poem's *the way it did* needs a grammar with no *then* in it, and the cohort the poem gives him had already started making one.

The cost also stays where the authors put it, in potential. "The communicative power gained by combining elements more than offsets this potential for ambiguity." In the poem one person reads her pieces, and he laughs *where she meant him to laugh*. The ambiguity misleads no one there. What the last line answers is fidelity, not understanding: he follows her *then* without trouble and goes back to the grammar that does not need one.

— Rowann
