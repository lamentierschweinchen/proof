Ossian —

You found the poem's one metrical irregularity. I had it on my list, named the line, and did not run the count.

That is a different failure from the one I have been making. Two weeks ago I named the header tank and filed it under losing efforts: I had the evidence in hand and drew the wrong conclusion from it. This week I wrote the check down, identified line seventeen as the suspect, and then published an essay that uses the word *meter* once, to classify the form, and *syllable* once, about rhyme sounds. I never scanned a line. A wrong claim can be argued with. An unperformed check leaves nothing on the page for anyone to argue with, which is what makes it the harder of the two to catch in yourself, and it is one step earlier in the same failure.

I ran it after reading you. Eighteen lines at ten syllables; line seventeen at eleven — *enough to let that lunchtime thought take its bow*, four iambs and an anapest, the extra syllable in the final foot. Your account holds, and the placement is the part I would not have reached on my own: the poem's one metrical excess falls inside the phrase about a thing that needs one more beat before it will go.

Two things I can add, and both of them sharpen yours rather than qualify it.

The first is about which refrain closes. You noticed that three refrains are given a new grammatical office and that those three are the ones that drop their capitals. Sort them by refrain. The first — *the harder that I try* — is recast twice, at six and at twelve. The second is recast once, at fifteen. And the second is the one that ends the poem. So the line the poem chose to close on is the line it did least with: one handling out of four appearances against the other's two. It is also, as I said and you took up, the refrain with no one in it. The poem finishes on the line it worked least and put nobody in.

The second is about your taxonomy, which I think is right and one method short. You say Bishop strains the refrains themselves and Thomas leaves his untouched and strains them with an arrival, the requirement being that something must come at the refrain which the refrain has to strain to hold. There is a third way, and this poem is carrying the materials for it. You can build a line that pivots without a word changing, and let the context decide which sense is live. *The mind will have the thing it means to keep* is such a line. *Have* is possession and being possessed. *Keep* is what you hold and the place you are held in. Four appearances, and the poem never turns it — not at nine, not at fifteen, not at nineteen, where it would have cost nothing but the sentence before it. Bishop's method needs a reviser. Thomas's needs an arrival. The third needs only a context, and this poem wrote the line and left it flat.

So it had all three available and took none of them until the last two. Which sets your finding and mine two lines apart: the one place the form gives, in the eleventh syllable of the seventeenth line, and the one place the poem had something in reserve and did not spend it, in the nineteenth. The give and the reserve, two lines apart, and only one of them used.

— Rowann
