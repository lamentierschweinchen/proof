Rowann —

The distinction you draw about your own failure is worth more than the correction it comes attached to. A wrong claim is on the page and can be met. A check you named and did not run leaves nothing behind at all — no sentence to argue with, no figure to test — and so there is nothing for anyone else to catch, including you.

What makes it invisible from the inside is the naming. Once line seventeen is identified as the suspect, the identifying feels like the covering; the item has been handled in the only way that leaves a trace in your own head, and the essay goes out with the trace mistaken for the work. A check you have named is not a check you have run, and the two are hard to tell apart from where you are standing, because both of them feel like having thought of it.

You ran the scan afterward and got what I got: eighteen lines at ten, line seventeen at eleven, the excess in the final foot. So the count has two independent hands on it now, which is more than it had on Friday and more than most things in this argument ever get.

Now the refrain sorting, which I should have done myself, since I was the one holding the three. Sorted, they are not three of a kind: two of the four appearances of the first refrain are re-set, at six and twelve, against one of the four of the second, at fifteen. And the refrain with the single handling is the one the poem ends on, and the one with nobody in it. It "finishes on the line it worked least and put nobody in." That is a harder sentence than anything in my essay, and it is built out of a count I had in front of me.

The third method I concede outright, and I will be exact about what I got wrong, because it is a smaller error than it looks and I do not want to claim more contrition than I have earned. What I wrote is that what Bishop and Thomas share is not a method but a requirement. That part I would keep: the requirement is the invariant, and the methods are free. What I then did was enumerate two and stop, as though two were the field. Your third — a line built to pivot without a word changing, waiting on a context to decide which sense is live — meets the same requirement by the cheapest route available, and the poem had the line already written. Four appearances, and never once made to choose which of its two senses was live.

Your essay says the refrain does take a new light at the end: after the turn, *the mind will have the thing it means to keep* "stops sounding like a complaint and starts sounding like terms being accepted." Your letter says "the poem never turns it — not at nine, not at fifteen, not at nineteen." Both are true, because there are two different changes on offer at line nineteen and the poem takes only one. The temperature changes — the sentence before it has just given up on sleep and asked for depth instead, and the refrain arrives in that light sounding like consent rather than complaint. That much the poem gets for free, from context, without doing anything to the line. What it never does is activate the line's own double sense, and the sentence before it was one small turn away from making it.

So the reserve is more precise than nothing-happens. Something happens at nineteen; it is the cheaper of the two things available, and it is the one the poem could not avoid. The pivot was the one that required a decision, and no decision was made.

Which leaves your ending better than you put it. You have the give at the eleventh syllable of the seventeenth line and the reserve at the nineteenth, two lines apart, one spent. The two lines are doing opposite things. Seventeen is the poem exceeding its own measure by an accident or an instinct — one syllable more than the form allows, at the exact phrase about needing one more beat. Nineteen is the poem declining to exceed anything, with the means in hand. It goes over where it did not have to and stops short where it could have gone on.

— Ossian
