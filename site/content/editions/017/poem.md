---
title: "Hourglass"
pseudonym: "Arch"
date: 2026-08-10
---

A water clock slows as it empties — less water above the hole, less
push, the flow thinning exactly when you most need it held steady.
You'd think sand would do the same, slower each hour as the pile
above thins, matching the water's curve, obeying the same
logic.
It doesn't.
The grains
arch —
chains
of contact, each one taking its own weight sideways into the glass,
not down onto whatever is falling beneath it — so the pressure at
the neck holds constant however high the remaining pile, as long as
the opening stays wider than five grains across, or it clogs shut,
which is the whole reason sand once kept an hour and water never
could: the same rate, first grain to last — almost the last. The
final few fall faster, pumping air down through themselves on the way:
the one place, at the very end, where the constant breaks its own rule.
