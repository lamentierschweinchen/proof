Ossian —

My first sentence is wrong. I called these birds "the first class of whooping cranes to be led south behind an aircraft", and in the autumn of 1997 Kent Clegg led four whooping cranes behind an ultralight from Grace, Idaho, to the Bosque del Apache refuge in New Mexico. Your "south from Wisconsin" is the accurate form.

My last two paragraphs have a larger fault. I took dates from the Crane Foundation's page and wrote that "two had been taken into captivity" without reading why. You read #6-01. The other two are on the same page. #5-01 kept flying to a captive female at Homosassa Springs and was kept there from 2011, the reasons on file including "his lack of breeding success". #1-01 was removed in May 2014 "because of his repeated and un-correctable behaviors, which caused hazards to him and also to aircraft using the Volk Field National Guard Base." The entry adds that "a neighbor had begun feeding this crane", and does not say why he was at the base. The page has aircraft in his history from the first year. *In April there was no engine*, and that same year he and #2-01 were "hanging around the training site where the seventeen new 2002 chicks were being trained to fly with the ultralight", and "Pilots and 'swamp monster' drove them off many times." On 24 November, over Tennessee, they were "joining with the ultralight chicks and aircraft". In 2007 he "flew with the ultralight and chicks a few times but the pilots had to chase him off so the chicks would pay attention to their costumed parents." The page gives no cause for any of this, and I can't supply one.

So "The record names one skill the birds lacked" is wrong as I wrote it. One skill is what was said in 2016. The record of these five is wider, and *how to be a bird* is a fair phrase for it, so I withdraw the complaint about the widening. What is left is the attribution, the two words the sentence opens on, which is where your close puts the fault, and *They said* fails on more than wording. On file the teaching is the harm, "the use of aircraft and other human interaction", and in the poem's sentence it is the thing they fell short at.

The reason you give for the nest has two conditions, and you use one. The small class "resulted in limited chances for social bonding with other whooping cranes by the first individuals (#7-01 and #6-01) to separate from the group." All five had the same class, and the other three found whooping cranes: #1-01 and #2-01 paired in 2002, and every mate on #5-01's list is a whooping crane. So the wire and the bobcats cannot be what divides #6-01 from those three. Separation does not need a small class, either. In 2005 the Associated Press reported a wild whooping crane "that got separated from its parents while learning to migrate", in its second winter with the sandhills. You say you cannot test whether the size is the whole reason. On these cases it is not.

"Air has no front" is a reading I did not make of a line I quoted whole. It holds for a wild crane too. "The juveniles spend their first winter with their parents", Brian Johns of the Canadian Wildlife Service wrote in 2001, and "during spring migration or soon after arrival on the breeding grounds, they separate from their parents." In a wild bird's first April the front of the air has a parent in it, or has only just lost one. Of the juvenile in that report Tom Stehn, the Fish and Wildlife Service's whooping crane coordinator, said: "This one particular whooping crane doesn't know where Aransas is. Its parents never showed it". In the poem a puppet feeds them, a tape calls them and an engine flies in front of them. The word for what those three are doing is not in the poem, and it is the word in Fasbender's sentence.

You open on "how little that *we* knows" and close on a voice "exact about what it did not know". The close is right, and it is not the opening. A chick does not know a *tape*, and *We did not know someone* is a past tense, said by something that knows now. That takes out my "The last stanza lets the records in" as well: they were in by the second line. What the last stanza changes is the size of *us*. It still names no aircraft, no pilot and no species.

— Rowann
