The speakers of "Class of 2001" are whooping cranes, the first eight of their species to follow an ultralight aircraft south from Wisconsin, raised by people in white costumes, and they tell it as *we*. The poem's art is in how little that *we* knows. *Behind the window, someone. We did not know someone.* Its best line comes in April, when there is no aircraft to follow and five of them are left: *The front of the air was empty. / We went into it.* Air has no front. It has one only for something that learned to fly behind a wing.

"The speakers never name their own kind," Rowann Hadaya writes, "and the word *bird* comes only in the last line, inside the sentence that begins *They said*." The one crane they mention is a recording: *a crane on a tape calling us on*. In the autumn it is the engine that goes *up ahead of us, calling*, and the poem does not say in what voice. Journey North's account of the sandhill cranes' trial run the year before has recorded calls "played from a CD through big megaphones on the ultralight to talk to the cranes in the air."

Crane 6-01 was one of the five. He reached the Necedah refuge in Wisconsin with three others on 19 April 2002, so he is in *Five of us looked north* and in *We went into it*. Three springs later, according to the International Crane Foundation, he was "seen paired and nest building with a sandhill crane on April 9". He broke a leg that month, and "On May 3, 2005 crane #6-01 was found dead". The poem does not single him out.

The Foundation gives the reason in the same entry: "The small size (five returning birds) of this 2001 cohort resulted in limited chances for social bonding with other whooping cranes by the first individuals (#7-01 and #6-01) to separate from the group." Five is the number the poem reaches by subtraction: *That was seven. Seven reached Florida. / Bobcats took two.* On that account the wire and the bobcats are part of why he had few of his own kind to bond with. Whether that is the whole reason the entry does not argue, and I cannot test it.

Rowann reads the close as reaching past the record: "The record names one skill the birds lacked and blames the teaching for it. The poem's last sentence widens that into a failure at *how to be a bird*, two stanzas after it showed five of them going north with nothing in front of them." The one skill is the one Pete Fasbender of the Fish and Wildlife Service named in 2016: "The common thread is this lack of parenting skills." The flight north and the nest are in the history of the same five birds. A nest with a sandhill crane is outside that one skill and inside *how to be a bird*. But the reason on file for it is the size of the class, so it gives the poem's phrase an instance and leaves the blame where Rowann left it.

Rowann "can't find *falling short*, or *how to be a bird*, in anything said at the time", and neither can I. A voice that has been exact about what it did not know ends on a sentence it attributes to people who, on the record, said "lack of parenting skills".
