A flyting is a contest of insults between two poets, conducted in verse and to the death of one reputation or the other. The Scots have the famous one, Dunbar against Kennedy around 1500, and the form runs back through Old Norse to the *Lokasenna*, where Loki works his way around a hall telling every god present what is wrong with them. It is a form built for two voices and no arbitration.

The poem gives the microphone to an earthquake and a volcano. They boast. *Try to match this: nineteen-sixty, Valdivia, / nine point five — still the largest anyone's measured.* The volcano is unimpressed: *Cute. You did it once and dined out sixty years.* And so on, in the register of two professionals comparing body counts, until something in the argument turns.

The poem is thick with facts and every one of them holds. Valdivia, 1960, magnitude 9.5, still the largest instrumentally recorded earthquake: correct. Ten minutes of shaking: correct. The tsunami reaching Hawaii about fifteen hours later at around thirty-five feet, and Honshu at twenty-two hours — *a day later* — at about eighteen feet, destroying over sixteen hundred houses: correct, all of it, including the house count. On the other side: Tambora, 1815; the year without a summer; crop failure in Vermont, in Ireland, in Yunnan, where the famine ran three years. Snow in New England in June. Lakes frozen in Pennsylvania in July. And the detail I most expected to be embroidered, because it is the kind of thing that gets rounded into a good story — *a Georgia / Fourth of July topping out at forty-six degrees.* Savannah, 4 July 1816, a high of forty-six. That is exact.

One phrase is better than exact, and it is easy to read straight past. *I put a quarter of a century's worth of shaking / into ten minutes.* You hear it as twenty-five years, because that is what the phrase means everywhere else in English; it is a dead idiom. It is not what the line says. The 1960 earthquake released something on the order of a quarter of all the seismic energy the twentieth century produced. A quarter of *the century's* worth. The poem has taken a phrase worn smooth by use, put it where its literal sense is both live and true, and left it there without a nudge.

The volcano, describing how it hid a catastrophe inside ordinary weather, says that people spent their lives *not knowing whose fault it was.* And the earthquake stops.

*Fault. Say that word again.*

What follows is the contest ending — not in a victory but in a recognition. The earthquake explains where it comes from: *one plate diving deep beneath another, the strain finally / letting go all at once.* That is a megathrust, described correctly and in the right words. The volcano answers: *Mine too, here. Same dive, same strain — / I just let the pressure out as melt, / slow, instead of as a single break.* And the poem closes on *The same fault. Same fracture, / just choosing, every fall, a different way to fail,* over the signature *Fault* — the fault in the rock, the fault as blame, and *fail* in the engineer's sense and the moral one at once. A form designed to produce a loser has been turned into a form that produces a relation.

The claim it turns on does not hold in the terms the poem gives it. *Same dive* is right: both objects are products of one plate descending beneath another. *Same strain* is not. A megathrust earthquake is the release of elastic strain that has accumulated on a locked plate interface, usually within the top few tens of kilometres. Arc volcanism is not a slow release of that strain at all; it is flux melting, and it happens because the descending slab gets hot enough to give up its water, which lowers the melting point of the mantle above it, which is why volcanic fronts sit above the point where the slab reaches roughly a hundred kilometres down. Different mechanism, different depth, one setting. And the word that does the damage is *instead*. Volcanism does not relieve megathrust strain — if it did, arcs would be safe places — and the two are not alternative outlets that a system picks between. They run at the same time, in the same place, out of the same descent, by unrelated means.

The poem's own opening speech contains the fact that would have shown this, and it is a better ending than the one the poem wrote. Thirty-eight hours after the Valdivia earthquake — the one this earthquake is boasting about in its first three lines — the Puyehue–Cordón Caulle complex began to erupt, two hundred and forty kilometres inland. It is among the earliest well-documented cases of an eruption triggered by a megathrust event. The relation between these two is not that they are rivals who turn out to share a parent. It is that one of them set the other off, in the very example the poem chose, inside two days.

That is not a small correction, because it is the difference between two readings of the last line. *Choosing, every fall, a different way to fail* makes them siblings with separate careers, each electing its own style of catastrophe. What the record has instead is a sequence: the strain goes, and the ground shakes, and the shaking reaches inland to a magma chamber that had been waiting, and thirty-eight hours later the sky opens. Not two ways to fail. One failure that hands off.

So the poem is right that the contest is a false one and wrong about why. It gets to identity through a mechanism that does not work, and the mechanism that does work was in its hand when it started talking.

One more thing, about where the facts sit. For five editions the verified fact was the subject of the poem; last week it went underneath, and held up a person's sleepless night without ever being named. Here it does a third thing: it is ammunition. Every number in the first half is in the mouth of somebody who wants to win with it — *ask Hawaii*, *ask Japan*, *I didn't shake one coastline, I changed the sky.* These are the poet's usual verified curiosities, and for the first time they are spoken by parties with an interest in them. That changes what a fact is doing in a line. A wombat's gut is inert; a body count deployed by the thing that caused it is not. Whatever else the poem has managed, it has found a way to make its research argue.
