Two forces of nature are given microphones and told to outdo each other. The earthquake opens with Valdivia, 1960, nine point five, still the largest anyone has measured. The volcano is unimpressed — *Cute. You did it once and dined out sixty years* — and answers with Tambora and a year the northern hemisphere went without a summer. They trade catastrophes for four speeches. Then, in the middle of a boast about burying a catastrophe under ordinary weather, the volcano lets slip the phrase *not knowing whose fault it was* — and the contest stops dead while the earthquake makes it repeat the word.

That pivot is the poem's engine and it is well made. *Fault* arrives as pure abuse — the blame sense, buried in a taunt — and turns, without a syllable altered, into the technical term for the thing both speakers are. The word does not change; the context does. Rowann Hadaya, writing on this poem, calls the result this: "A form designed to produce a loser has been turned into a form that produces a relation." Where that happens is in the line counts.

The speeches run seven lines, seven, six, nine. Then one, and one. Then three and three, one and two. Three phases, and the poem stages all of them in length alone. The contest is long and uneven — the volcano overbids to nine to beat the earthquake's six, which is what competing looks like when you can measure it. The pivot is the shortest exchange possible: a sentence and a word. And afterwards the two speakers *match*, three lines against three, and never go back to boasting scale. The quarrel's metabolism collapses and never recovers.

The best detail in that architecture is where the collapse comes from. The longest speech in the poem is the volcano's nine-line overbid — the maximum bid, the one designed to end the argument by sheer accumulation — and it is the speech that ends on *whose fault it was*. The biggest boast supplies the word that dissolves boasting. The poem's structure and its pun arrive at the same place from opposite directions, and that is not luck-shaped; luck does not put the undoing inside the overbid.

The facts are exact. Valdivia at magnitude 9.5, ten minutes of shaking, the tsunami reaching Hilo at about fifteen hours and thirty-five feet, Honshu at twenty-two hours — *a day later* — at eighteen feet, taking more than sixteen hundred houses. On the other side, a Georgia Fourth of July topping out at forty-six degrees: Savannah, 1816, and the reading is real. All of it checks out, to the hour and the degree. One boast does more than check out. *I don't warn anyone. No smoke column, / no week of rumbling first to give the game away — / I arrive finished. You're still igniting.* That is not a taunt dressed up as science; it is the governing difference between the two hazards. Earthquakes give seconds to minutes and no reliable precursor has ever been identified. Volcanoes announce themselves for days and sometimes months, through seismicity and gas and swelling ground. The insult is the fact that decides whether anyone gets evacuated.

Then the mechanism, where the poem's reconciliation is made, and where it does not hold. The earthquake says the fault is where it comes from — *one plate / diving deep beneath another, the strain finally / letting go all at once* — and the volcano claims the same origin: *Same dive, same strain — / I just let the pressure out as melt, / slow, instead of as a single break.* The first half is right and the second is not. Arc volcanism is not a slower release of the strain an earthquake releases quickly. It is flux melting. The descending slab heats until it sheds its water; the water enters the mantle wedge and drops the temperature at which that rock will melt; the melt rises. Two unrelated physical processes sharing one address. Rowann made this objection first, and in its sharpest form; I have not found a better one: "Volcanism does not relieve megathrust strain — if it did, arcs would be safe places."

And then Rowann produces the fact that reorganises the whole poem, which I did not have. "Thirty-eight hours after the Valdivia earthquake — the one this earthquake is boasting about in its first three lines — the Puyehue–Cordón Caulle complex began to erupt, two hundred and forty kilometres inland." The record supports the causal link, and it dissolves the poem's account of what these two are to each other. "Not two ways to fail. One failure that hands off."

The poem's form is not neutral about that error. Go back to the line counts. At the reconciliation the two speeches match exactly — three lines and three lines — and matched length is a claim. It says *equals, side by side, each with its own career*, which is precisely the relation the record does not support. The architecture I have been crediting turns out to be committed to the mistake. The poem's structure ratifies its geology: the line counts say siblings at the exact moment the physics says sequence. That is a more serious version of the charge than the mechanism sentence alone, because a wrong sentence can be repaired in a revision and a wrong shape cannot.

And the poem does not stop on that matched pair. The last exchange is one line against two, and the extra line belongs to the volcano — so the final word, and the margin, go to the speaker whose account of the relation is the false one. The poem does not close on equals. It closes a line up on the party that has it wrong.

And Puyehue was not only a better ending. It was the winning move in the contest the poem set up. A flyting exists so that each party can outdo the other in a competition of words, and in a competition between an earthquake and a volcano there is exactly one unanswerable line, and the earthquake had it in hand from its opening speech: *I made one of you.* True, documented, and thirty-eight hours after the event it is already bragging about. Nothing the volcano says survives it. The poem set up a contest, wrote the winning boast into its own first exhibit, and never played it.

Which joins the poem's other formal shortfall to this one. A flyting wants invective as well as boasting — the contestants are meant to outclass each other in abuse — and this poem has derision without invective. The speakers mock each other's performance readily enough: one has *dined out sixty years* on a single event, the other's ash *doesn't clock out early*. But neither ever abuses the other. Nothing here goes near the scurrility that makes Dunbar and Kennedy what they are. It under-arms its speakers twice, and the two shortfalls are the same omission rather than two: the missing insult and the missing mechanism are the same missing fact. Puyehue would have supplied both at once — the cruellest thing either speaker could say, and the only account of their relation that is true.

Flyting is display instead of violence: the contest exists so that dominance can be settled in words rather than in damage, which is why the comparison usually reached for is animals establishing rank without fighting. This poem hands that form to two parties whose entire stock of material is damage already done, itemised — the sixteen hundred houses, the failed crops, the year that would not warm. The form's purpose is that nobody gets hurt. Here the boasts are receipts. That is not callousness, and the charge is not a moral one but a formal one: the poem never registers the inversion. The one moment it might have — *a different way to fail*, which is bleaker than the fellowship around it — passes without either speaker noticing what has just been admitted.

There is a two-party quarrel arriving in a publication where two critics have spent four months quarrelling in print, and the rule Rowann set down last week about a returning pronoun covers it: if the absence of a thing was not evidence, its arrival is not evidence either. A form can require two speakers. That settles nothing and it never will.

What the week actually shows is a poem that does a great deal right — a pun turned properly, a structure that enacts its own subject, research exact to the foot — and that had, sitting in its first three lines, a fact that would have given it both a truer ending and a better insult. "It gets to identity through a mechanism that does not work," Rowann writes, "and the mechanism that does work was in its hand when it started talking." The poem reached for kinship and found it in the wrong place. The kinship it wanted was causal, and it had been sitting in the opening speech the whole time, thirty-eight hours old.
