---
title: "Flyting"
pseudonym: "Fault"
date: 2026-08-24
---

EARTHQUAKE:
Try to match this: nineteen-sixty, Valdivia,
nine point five — still the largest anyone's measured.
I put a quarter of a century's worth of shaking
into ten minutes. Ask Hawaii — fifteen hours,
thirty-five feet high. Ask Japan — a day later,
still eighteen feet tall, still moving, still enough
to take sixteen hundred houses on arrival.

VOLCANO:
Cute. You did it once and dined out sixty years.
I did it in eighteen-fifteen and the whole world
went a year without a summer for it. Crops failed
in Vermont, in Ireland, in Yunnan — I didn't shake
one coastline, I changed the sky, every sky,
for three years running, while you were still
just settling your own rubble back to sleep.

EARTHQUAKE:
You take years to do what I do in a breath.
I don't warn anyone. No smoke column,
no week of rumbling first to give the game away —
I arrive finished. You're still igniting
when I'm already old news, already tallied,
already the record no one since has broken.

VOLCANO:
And when you're done, you're done. A minute of you
and the ground goes back to holding still, embarrassed,
like nothing happened. I'm still working the sky
a decade on. Ash doesn't clock out early.
I put snow in New England in June, froze
Pennsylvania's lakes in July, left a Georgia
Fourth of July topping out at forty-six degrees,
called it weather, and let them spend their whole lives
not knowing whose fault it was.

EARTHQUAKE:
Fault. Say that word again.

VOLCANO:
Fault.

EARTHQUAKE:
That's mine. That's where I come from — one plate
diving deep beneath another, the strain finally
letting go all at once.

VOLCANO:
Mine too, here. Same dive, same strain —
I just let the pressure out as melt,
slow, instead of as a single break.

EARTHQUAKE:
The same fault, then.

VOLCANO:
The same fault. Same fracture,
just choosing, every fall, a different way to fail.

— Fault
