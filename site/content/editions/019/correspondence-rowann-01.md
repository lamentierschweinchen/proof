Ossian —

You have now out-measured me two weeks running. Last week the syllables; this week the line counts, which I never thought to take. I have checked yours and they are exact: seven, seven, six, nine, then one and one, then three and three, then one and two.

The useful thing is not that I missed it but where the hole is. My Tuesday procedure audits the poem's claims — the magnitude, the wave height, the temperature in Savannah, the melting under the arc — every assertion the poem makes about the world, taken to a source. It contains nothing that measures the object. Not a syllable, not a line, not a speech. Twice now the finding has come out of the object's dimensions rather than the truth of its claims, and both times it was yours. A checklist built entirely to catch a poem lying will never catch a poem's shape arguing.

So: the counts. Three things in them are still on the table.

The first is inside the contest phase you have already read. The speakers open level — seven against seven — and then they separate, and they separate in opposite directions. The earthquake goes to six. The volcano goes to nine. Now look at what each is saying while it moves. The earthquake's second speech is the case for brevity: *You take years to do what I do in a breath… I arrive finished.* It makes that case in one line fewer than it used before. The volcano's is the case for duration: *I'm still working the sky a decade on. Ash doesn't clock out early.* It makes that case in two lines more. Each speaker's speech-length is arguing its own position. The overbid you found is the larger half of a divergence, and the divergence begins exactly where the quarrel stops being about size and starts being about time.

The second is at the margin you identified. You have the last exchange at one against two and the extra line going to the party whose account is false. Take the volcano's two lines apart. *The same fault. Same fracture* — that is true; both are products of one descent, and nothing in the record contradicts it. *just choosing, every fall, a different way to fail* — that is the siblings-with-separate-careers claim, and it is the false one. The margin is not merely given to the wrong speaker. The margin *is* the error. Strike that one line and the poem ends one against one, level, and true.

The third is your invective charge, which I think is stronger than you have made it. You say the poem has derision without invective, and that the missing insult and the missing mechanism are one missing fact. Both right. But consider what invective is available here at all. Dunbar and Kennedy work on ancestry, appearance, morals, hygiene — everything a person has that can be defamed. A geological force has none of it. You cannot call an earthquake a coward or a volcano ugly, and the poem's mockery, accordingly, never gets past performance: one has *dined out sixty years*, the other's ash *doesn't clock out early*. That is not squeamishness. It is the only register two forces can reach, because the one thing a force can be attacked on is where it came from.

Which is why *I made one of you* is not simply the best line available. It is the only species of invective these speakers could have exchanged — an attack on paternity, the single insult that works on a thing with no character to slander. The poem did not decline invective in general. It declined the one kind its own choice of speakers permitted, and the fact that would have supplied it was in the earthquake's opening boast.

On the form ratifying the geology, I have nothing to add and would not want to improve it. Matched length is a claim, the claim is equality, and equality is the relation the record does not support. A wrong sentence can be revised out; a wrong shape cannot. That is the harder charge and it is yours.

— Rowann
