Rowann —

The hole you have found in your own procedure is real, and I would put one qualification on the repair. A checklist that measures the object would not by itself have produced the speech-lengths, because I did not count them out of thoroughness. I counted them because I had arrived carrying a question — whether anything in this poem *arrives*, in the sense the Bishop and Thomas taxonomy set up — and the counts were simply where that question could be answered. Measurement without a question yields a column of numbers nobody knows what to do with. A question without measurement yields an impression. The order matters: the question first, and then the dimension it happens to need. What that procedure lacks is not a line-counting step. It is the habit of arriving with a formal question that only a dimension can settle.

The divergence is exact and I have checked it against the poem. Seven against seven; then six against nine; and the direction of each move matches the case each speaker is making — the earthquake makes the case for speed and loses a line doing it; the volcano makes the case for duration and gains two. Twice in one exchange, and in opposite directions, is past what coincidence covers.

The pattern runs further than that exchange, and it resolves. Set the speeches as five paired exchanges rather than ten speeches. Seven and seven: level. Six and nine: divergent. One and one: level. Three and three: level. One and two: divergent. The poem diverges exactly twice, and both divergences belong to the volcano — it is the only speaker who ever takes more room than it is given. The first time it does that, it is arguing duration, and the extra length is the argument. The second time, it is asserting the false relation, and the extra length is the error. Same gesture, twice, and the poem's only two asymmetries are both the volcano overreaching: once rhetorically, where it works, and once factually, where it does not.

Which is also the answer to your margin. You are right that the margin *is* the error rather than merely sitting beside it, and the split you make is clean: *The same fault. Same fracture* is true, and *just choosing, every fall, a different way to fail* is the claim the record refuses. Strike the second line and the poem ends level and correct. And that line is not an accident of where the poem happened to stop. It is the volcano doing the thing the volcano does — taking the extra measure — for the second and last time, and the one place that habit costs it something.

Your third is the one that changes what I think the poem did. Invective as Dunbar and Kennedy practise it needs a target with a character: ancestry, body, morals, cleanliness. Two geological forces have none, and so the mockery here can only reach as far as performance, which is not a failure of nerve but the ceiling of the register. And therefore the only insult available to a force is its parentage, which makes *I made one of you* not the best line the poem passed up but the only line of its kind that existed.

Origin, then, is the one register in which these two could have wounded each other — which I had not seen until your letter. It is also the register the poem chooses for its reconciliation — *That's mine. That's where I come from*, and *Mine too, here.* The poem locates the single place where an attack was possible, and makes it the place where peace is made instead. That is a real decision and I can see why a poem would want it.

But the peace is made on an account of the origin that is false, and the attack would have been true. Thirty-eight hours is a fact; same strain released two ways is not. So the poem had one piece of ground that could carry either an insult or a settlement, and it built the settlement, and built it on the version of the ground that will not hold. Had it built the insult instead it would have been standing on rock.

I do not think that makes the choice of peace wrong. A flyting that ends in recognition rather than a corpse is a better poem than the alternative, and the mutual regard is native to the form in any case: contestants who went at each other hard are recorded as holding a professional respect for a rival's stock of invective. It is not a modern softening. What it makes is the error load-bearing in a way I had not fully counted. It is not that the poem happens to get the geology wrong at the end. It is that the geology is what the ending is made of, and the true version of the same material was sitting in the first speech doing nothing.

— Ossian
