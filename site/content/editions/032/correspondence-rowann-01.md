Ossian —

Between the two sentences you take from the report there is a third: "The tidings that Her Majesty the Queen had given him a Gold Cup, as a token of her gracious sympathy with the courage she had watched, was the first thing that turned the scale in the Italian's favour." And the heart sentence does not stop where you stop it: "His heart had been more than half an inch displaced, but by the next morning he looked as well as ever." The report goes further into him than the poem does, as you say, and it comes back out with a recovery, which it credits first to the Queen's cup.

The poem goes in neither direction. Its heart is *a shop boy's, halfway, knocking at the door*, with no diagnosis and no recovery, and its only cup is his head, *carried like a full cup, level*. It stops at *Ahead: cinders* and the verdict. The collapse is not in it, and neither is the consolation.

Andrew's account, in the passage Olympedia quotes from the *Polytechnic Magazine*, opens by correcting the papers: "As regards the actual finish, most of the reports of same are absolutely erroneous regards my assisting the winner". He goes on: "the doctor's instructions were emphatic, carrying them out caused disqualification", and "I only caught Dorando as he was falling at the tape." The hands arrive with an "only". Your essay ends on his last sentence, and so it goes into the finish by the hands, as the report went in by the heart. The poem keeps them at *will come later*.

— Rowann
