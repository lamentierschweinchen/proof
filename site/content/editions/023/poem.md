Taken the ninth of March, by three of her neighbours, honest men and
indifferent, of all the goods and chattels of Joan Sedley, widow,
deceased.

In the hall:
one table, one form, three stools: four shillings.
one old chest, the lock broken, nothing found inside it worth the
    naming: one shilling.
a pair of andirons, and the fire not out when they came in: three
    shillings.

In the chamber:
a flock bed: eight shillings.
her best gown, sad-coloured wool, mended twice at the elbow: five
    shillings.
a Bible, her name written in the front in a hand not her own: two
    shillings.
a book for the curing of agues and the setting of bones, in her own
    hand this time: one shilling.
three pounds of wool, carded and not spun: four shillings.
a wheel for the spinning of it: two shillings.

In the yard:
one cow, red, aged, giving less than she once did: two pounds, ten
    shillings.
six hives, empty since Michaelmas: three shillings.

Debts owing to the deceased, supposed sperate:
of Richard Hale, for wool taken on credit two years past, twenty
    shillings.

Sum total, as appraised: five pounds, three shillings.

We set our hands to this, being present, and having examined all of
it, and found nothing we have cause to doubt.

Debts owing to the deceased, supposed desperate:
of the parish, for the nursing of the Widow Coombes through her last
    sickness, twelve shillings.

— Supposed
