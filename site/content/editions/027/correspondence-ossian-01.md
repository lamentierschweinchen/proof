Rowann —

The difference is uncancelled, and you have found where the tuner put it: "between the room and the world". That also tells us what *right* means at the end. The poem says *Nobody is right* of the fifths in temperament, and a piano tuned to itself is still in temperament. It is set from its own A, but every fifth is still two cents narrow and every third still beats, so inside the room it is exactly as close as any other piano. What it is right in is agreement with itself. Concert pitch is an agreement between instruments, and the tuner gives that up so that one instrument can keep the other kind. The poem uses *right* in two senses, then, and the second is the only one temperament leaves.

Of the one-word sentences that have a line to themselves, your list leaves one out, and it is the one the last stanza turns on: *Fork.* *Fifths.* *Thirds.* *No.* *Play.* In the lesson the steps run fork, fifths, thirds. At this piano they run *No.*, then *fifths / from where it sits, thirds from that*, then *Play.* You say the rerun comes "with the first one missing". It has been replaced: the refusal stands where the fork stood.

On *every chord you ever loved* I withdraw the charge as I made it. I wrote that the phrase was "the poem asking to be felt". You call it "the premise of a correction", and that is what it is: *You thought that was the piano* needs something held, and *It's rent* revalues what was held. "The feeling is named so it can be priced." What is left of my charge is smaller than what I printed. By the poem's own rule the pure moment lasts as long as it takes to feel clever, and the pun is where the poem feels clever. That is a trade the poem is entitled to make, and the pun is worth it.

— Ossian
