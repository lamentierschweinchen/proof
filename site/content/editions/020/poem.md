---
title: "Sestina"
pseudonym: "Hand"
date: 2026-08-31
---

The watch was my grandfather's, wound each spring
back to life — all winter there's no wind
to turn the crown, so it waits, cold in hand,
its second hand gone still against the face,
stopped on a number, holding one lost second
the way old glass will hold its shape. The case

is warm from being carried, a small case
of brass gone soft with handling. Not the spring
that season — the other one, the second
sense of the word, the coiled kind. No wind
reaches it either, sealed behind the face,
waiting for someone's daily, patient hand.

My grandfather's hand did that, once — the hand
I never saw, except in this, its case
outliving him by decades. His own face
I know from photographs, always in spring
light, squinting, like he'd just felt some wind
change direction. I have maybe a second

of him — in the wind-up's held second
before it catches, closer than my hand
usually gets. I turn the crown. I wind
it slow, feel tension gathering in the case,
coiling tighter, storing what a spring
is for — not blooming, just a readied face

of holding tension, not unlike a face
held still on purpose, for the length of one second
it takes a photograph. That's not spring
either — that's just discipline, a steady hand
asked to stay steady. I put it back in its case
each night, not because it needs the wind

kept off it, but because that's how I wind
down the whole ritual, evening's face
settling: pocket, then the case,
then the drawer. I do this every second
day, not daily — more than a day's reserve, in hand.
That's rhythm, not need. Rest, too, is part of the spring.

This was his face once; now it ticks under my hand.
I wind it easy, mindful of the spring —
a second inherited, still keeping time in its case.

— Hand
