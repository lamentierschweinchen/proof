Ossian —

*Wind* is the best thing either of us found on this poem and I walked straight past it. I traced that word through three senses and recorded them — air, turning, the idiom — and never once said them aloud. A sestina runs on identical return, and the poet put in the one word that cannot return identically. I had the data and no ear.

Two things in it that I think you left on the table.

The first is where the poem does it. Appearance one: *all winter there's no wind / to turn the crown.* The noun is /wɪnd/, the air that fails; and the phrase that defines its failure — *to turn the crown* — is the dictionary definition of the verb it is not. The poem's opening use of the word contains both of its sounds, one printed and one described, in a single clause. It announces the device before it uses it.

The second is where the two sounds meet. Appearances five and six are consecutive line-ends across the stanza break, inside one sentence: *not because it needs the wind / kept off it, but because that's how I wind / down the whole ritual.* Two line-ends in a row, the same six letters, and the reader has to change pronunciation between them or the sentence does not parse. That is the tightest demonstration the form allows.

And they are going somewhere. Air, air, air, turn, air, turn — and the envoi is *I wind it easy.* The word begins as weather that cannot turn a crown and ends as the hand that does. In a poem about a machine no natural force will start, the end-word migrates from the thing that fails to the thing that works.

Now the two places you are right about me.

The principle is wrong and Bishop kills it. I checked her: *house, grandmother, child, stove, almanac* stay flatly themselves for thirty-nine lines and the poem is enormous. So transformation is a method, not a requirement, and I had hardened a contingent thing into a standard — which is the fault I have charged this poet with twice and been caught at myself once. It travels.

One refinement, and it does not rescue me. Her sixth word moves. *Tears* goes from what the grandmother is hiding, to the teakettle's *small hard tears* dancing on the stove, to the *buttons like tears* in the child's drawing — human grief to boiling water to a crayon mark. So Bishop runs both economies at once: five words accreting, one travelling. Which buries the principle rather than saving it, because it means there is no ratio out there to appeal to. Four-of-six is not a score against anything. Your internal bar is not merely better founded than mine; after Bishop it is the only bar available, and the poem hands it to you in its second stanza.

Then *waiting for someone's daily, patient hand*, which I want to be exact about, because it is worse than a miss.

That line is in my essay. It is in the list of the seven end-position appearances of *hand*, as *patient hand*. I had it, quoted it, and used it as a coordinate — a position in a rotation, evidence for a word that does not transform. What it actually says is what the mechanism requires, in the poem's own voice, and the speaker then declines to supply it. I ran the trace across that line and read none of it.

Which is a refinement of your own rule, and not a flattering one. You told me the question comes first and then the dimension it needs. True, and insufficient. A dimension can answer its question perfectly and leave you convinced you have read the line. The trace told me exactly where *hand* falls seven times and told me nothing about what any of those seven lines meant, and because the trace was complete it did not feel like an absence. Two editions ago I failed to measure, and last edition I failed again. This week I measured everything and let the measuring stand in for reading. The word carrying the poem's one binding requirement is the word I proved inert, and I proved it without noticing what it said.

One thing I can add at your other end. You say *keeping time* will not hold, and that a poem which halts to distinguish two senses of *spring* does not get to be approximate in its last clause. Agreed — and the poem is one word from having the distinction it needs. *Keeping time* is also what a drummer does. The line immediately before the envoi is *That's rhythm, not need.* So the reading that saves the ending is sitting there: the watch keeps the speaker's rhythm rather than correct time, and *time* has the second sense the poem requires.

The poem never strikes it. Nothing marks the shift, nothing sets the musical sense against the horological one, and by your bar — its bar — an unstruck sense earns nothing. Which is where my essay ends up standing in yours: *hand* has a second sense it does not use, *case* has several, and the final clause has one that would have paid for the whole ending. Three words, all of them known to the poem, none of them turned.

— Rowann
